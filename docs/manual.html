SpeechChem Manual

Welcome! This guide walks you through the first two SpaceChem tutorials with SpeechChem, and is meant to replace the shoddy in-game version. A key reference for everything else is at the end.

1. The basics

1.1 The grid

SpaceChem's research levels, which is what you start with, are played on a 10 by 8 grid. Coordinates are spoken column first, then row, counting from 1 at the top left: (1, 1) is the top-left corner, (10, 8) the bottom-right one. For a start, think of the grid as three zones:

  • Inputs, on the left: alpha (α) from (1, 1) to (4, 4), beta (β) from (1, 5) to (4, 8). New molecules appear here.
  • Outputs, on the right: psi (ψ) from (7, 1) to (10, 4), omega (ω) from (7, 5) to (10, 8). Finished molecules leave from here.
  • The chamber: the narrow vertical strip in between, columns 5 and 6.

There are exceptions to the above, but I'm sure you'll figure them out. When you cross from one zone into another, the grid tells you ("alpha input", "chamber", "psi output").

1.2 Waldos

You don't actually get to do any chemistry. Instead, you control two waldos: robotic arms that travel across the grid under your guidance, one red and one blue. The basic idea is simple: grab atoms, make chemistry happen, deliver them to the output zones, profit. Each waldo follows only the instructions of its own colour.

Each cell can hold one arrow and one non-arrow instruction per colour. When a waldo passes over a cell, its instruction runs first, then its arrow.

2. The reactor screen

The reactor screen is split into stops; Tab and Shift+Tab move between them, and they wrap.

  1. Grid: where you start. Arrows move one cell. Each cell reads its coordinates first, then the zone when you cross into a new one, then what's in it: instructions by colour, and during a run the waldos and atoms. An empty cell reads just its coordinates.
  2. Palette: the instructions this level allows, each with its key letter ("E, grab drop"). You rarely need to visit it: the letters work straight from the grid.
  3. Layers: which colour is active (the colour new instructions get), and whether each colour is visible or locked.
  4. Tools: the toolbar: stop, step, the play buttons, undo and redo, the periodic table and so on.
  5. Molecules: the inputs and outputs, with what each one takes or wants and how many you've delivered ("psi output: Oxygen, O2, 0 of 10"). Enter opens a molecule.
  6. Status: the level's progress.
  7. Tutorial: the current tutorial step, only while there is one. Ctrl+T repeats it from anywhere. The cell the tutorial wants reads "highlighted".
  8. Run log: every event of the last run, grouped by cycle; it appears once a run has logged something.

3. Tutorial 1: Of Pancakes and Spaceships

3.1 Where things start

Your waldos start at (5, 2) and (5, 7). Shift+R and Shift+B read where they are, and Ctrl+Shift+R / Ctrl+Shift+B also move your cursor there. They'll generally start there unless you move the start markers around, which you don't need to do here. Jump to the red waldo and you'll hear 5, 2, red start left. It starts moving left. Hopefully it's now clear why the tutorial first asks you to go to (4, 2), one cell to the left, and place the "in" instruction, which you do with R. When the waldo passes over it, a new molecule appears in the input zone, and the waldo goes on its merry way.

3.2 The molecule

Where does said molecule appear, you ask? Press M on any cell of the alpha zone, and an overlay of the input opens with your cursor on the molecule. Here it says Oxygen, O2, followed by 2, 2, Oxygen, double bond right. That tells us a few things:

  • We're picking up oxygen (duh). That's what we want: the Molecules stop says psi wants 10 of it, psi output: Oxygen, O2, 0 of 10.
  • The atom has a double bond to its neighbour. Ignore that bit for now.
  • The bond points right.

Pressing Right then gets you 3, 2, Oxygen, double bond left. So the molecule runs west to east from (2, 2) to (3, 2), two oxygen atoms doubly bonded to each other. Escape closes the overlay.

3.3 Building the program

Having spawned the molecule, we need to go and grab it. The tutorial helpfully suggests a grab / drop instruction (E) at (3, 2). Why there? Because after the "in" at (4, 2), your waldo is still moving left, and (3, 2) is the next cell, the one with the right-hand oxygen in it.

Having picked up the molecule by one end (move one atom and the other comes along, since they're bonded), we need to turn around and deliver it to the output zone in the top-right. The tutorial suggests an arrow down (S) at (2, 2). When the waldo hits it, it turns and heads down the second column, (2, 2) to (2, 3) to (2, 4), and two rows down is plenty, so the next stop is an arrow right (D) at (2, 4).

Our waldo now heads right, out of the alpha input, through the chamber and into the psi output. For demonstration purposes, the tutorial has you place a rotate counter-clockwise instruction (G) at (6, 4), right on that path. It does what you think: when a waldo holding a molecule crosses it, the molecule turns 90 degrees to the left.

Next comes an arrow up (W) at (9, 4). The goal in every level is a loop that keeps delivering the molecule you want, over and over. Matching what we did on the left, it shouldn't come as a surprise that we then turn left (A) at (9, 2). But before we head home…

To make a delivery, we must:

  • drop the molecule so that all of it is inside the output zone, and
  • pass over an output instruction for that zone.

Which is exactly what the tutorial asks for: a grab / drop (E) at (8, 2) and an output (F) at (7, 2).

3.4 Checking the path

Congratulations! You've just built a loop for your red sla—I mean, friendly machine. To hear where and how it will travel, press Ctrl+R:

3.5 Stepping through the run

Oh, the beauty of the process, the elegance, the perfection of the machi—I mean, right. We probably want to run the reactor now. Any of the keys 1 to 4 will do that, but first, allow me to talk about stepping.

Press 0 and the reactor runs to the next interesting event, then pauses. An event is interesting if it's more than plain movement: a molecule coming in, a grab or drop, a turn, a rotation, an output, a waldo hitting the wall. What you hear is what happened in the cycle that just ran. Press 0 now:

Cycle 1

red: in alpha, Oxygen, O2 at 2, 2

Arrow around and you'll find the red waldo at 3, 2, red grab drop, red waldo, facing left, Oxygen, double bond left. In other words, it passed the "in", which brought the molecule in, and it's still heading left.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 2

red: grabbed Oxygen, O2

The waldo is at (2, 2) now, right after the grab: 2, 2, red arrow down, red waldo, facing left, Oxygen, double bond left. Importantly, it brought the other atom with it. Arrow to (1, 2) and you'll hear 1, 2, Oxygen, double bond right. If the waldo kept going left, that left-hand atom would try to leave the reactor. That's generally undesirable, and you will be sad, because the reactor will throw an error and stop.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 3

red: heading down

The waldo is now at 2, 3, red waldo, facing down, Oxygen, double bond left.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 4

blue: hit the wall at 1, 7

Ah, yes. Your other waldo! Both waldos run at the same time. Since you didn't tell the blue one to do anything special, it set off left from (5, 7) (Shift+B to check) and kept going until it smacked into the wall. It will now sit there for the rest of the run and feel left out.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 5

red: heading right

Pretty standard stuff at this point. The waldo is at 3, 4, red waldo, facing right, Oxygen, double bond left.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 9

red: rotate counter-clockwise, rotated Oxygen, O2

Something interesting! Go check it out: the waldo is at 6, 4, red rotate counter-clockwise, red waldo, facing right, Oxygen, double bond down, and as the bond suggests, the rotation happened. The oxygen now sits at (6, 4) and (6, 5). Note the cycle numbers jumped from 5 to 9: cycles without events pass silently.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 13

red: heading up

Rotating the molecule means we now have to either rotate it back or head up, as we just did. Remember, the whole molecule must fit inside the output zone. Psi spans (7, 1) to (10, 4), so entering it on row 4 with one oxygen hanging below on row 5 leaves that atom dangling in omega's territory. Anyway, we're at (9, 3) now, heading up.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 15

red: heading left

Press 0 again:

Cycle 16

red: dropped Oxygen, O2

Press 0 again:

Cycle 17

red: out psi

Research Output ψ: Oxygen, O2, 1 of 10

And there we have it! We just have to press 0 about twenty more times to…

I'm kidding. You can keep stepping with 0, but if you're bored, press 1 to hear the events in real time at the slowest speed. Press 4 to race through at top speed, which completes the level. 2 and 3 are for when you want to skip some cycles without being spammed by speech: events are only spoken at speed 1 and while stepping. Everything is always in the Run log, though.

And with that, level 1 is complete!

3.6 When things go wrong

Sooner or later, a reactor of yours will crash. The tutorial even warns you: "If an atom collides with another atom or the side of the reactor the simulation will be stopped!" Let's do it on purpose.

Go to (2, 2) and press Delete to remove the arrow down (with red active; Delete removes the active colour's instructions). Now the waldo grabs the oxygen and just keeps going left. Step with 0:

Cycle 1

red: in alpha, Oxygen, O2 at 2, 2

Cycle 2

red: grabbed Oxygen, O2

So far, so good. Press 0 again:

Paused

Reaction Error

Atoms cannot leave the bounds of a reactor. The reaction will now be stopped.

At 1, 2

Okay, button

The waldo dragged the molecule into the wall, and the left-hand oxygen tried to leave the reactor. The game pauses and shows an error box: what went wrong, and the cell where it happened. Press Enter on Okay and the run stops. Everything resets: molecules gone, waldos back at their starts. So how do you find out what the reactor looked like at the moment of the crash?

The Run log. It's the last stop, so Shift+Tab from the grid gets you there:

Run log, Cycle 4, Paused, 1 of 3

The log holds every event of the last run, grouped by cycle. Up and Down move through the events, Ctrl+Up and Ctrl+Down jump a whole cycle, and Home and End go to the start and end of the log. When a cycle has several events, each says its position in it. Arrow up through it and you'll hear the whole story:

Cycle 1, red: in alpha, Oxygen, O2 at 2, 2

Cycle 2, red: grabbed Oxygen, O2

Cycle 4, Paused, 1 of 3

Reaction Error: Atoms cannot leave the bounds of a reactor. The reaction will now be stopped., button, 2 of 3

Stopped, 3 of 3

Notice the error is a button. Press Enter on it and you get the reactor exactly as it was when it crashed, frozen, with the cursor on the cell the error box marked:

Reactor at cycle 4

1, 2, alpha input, red waldo, facing left, Oxygen, double bond left, error here

There's our culprit: the waldo is at the left edge, still facing left, holding an oxygen whose bond points left, out of the reactor. Its partner had nowhere to go. You can arrow around this frozen copy like the real grid (every instruction is there too, all layers), and Shift+Backspace gives a cell's details:

red waldo, holding Oxygen, O2. Oxygen, atomic number 8, Maximum Bonds: 2

Escape takes you back to the log. The log is cleared when you start a new run, so take a look before you do.

Now put the arrow back: Ctrl+Z undoes the delete, or press S on (2, 2).

4. Tutorial 2: Slightly Different

This time the red waldo gets the day off. You'll take hydrogen from the beta input and deliver 10 of it to the omega output, using the blue waldo. The tutorial (and this guide) walks you through half the program and then leaves the rest to you.

4.1 The molecule

Move to any cell of the beta zone, (1, 5) to (4, 8), and press M. You'll hear Hydrogen, H2, and the cursor lands on 2, 6, Hydrogen, single bond right. Right from there gives 3, 6, Hydrogen, single bond left. So: two hydrogen atoms, side by side from (2, 6) to (3, 6), joined by a single bond. The Molecules stop confirms what omega wants: omega output: Hydrogen, H2, 0 of 10.

4.2 Going blue

The blue waldo behaves exactly like the red one, but follows only blue instructions.

Instructions of the other colour don't exist as far as a waldo is concerned. The red waldo can run straight over blue instructions and nothing happens, and the other way round. So the two colours can share cells and even whole paths without getting in each other's way. (The waldos themselves don't collide either. Atoms do.)

The tutorial's first step only checks that the blue start is at (5, 7). It is: that's where it starts anyway. Shift+B says so.

4.3 The tutorial's half

Our blue waldo heads left from (5, 7), so the first instruction goes at (3, 7): an input. Press R there and you'll hear red in alpha. Wrong on both counts: we want a blue instruction, and our hydrogen comes from beta. There are no separate keys for those: you place the instruction and change it. Two ways:

  • The context menu: Backspace on the cell opens the instruction's menu (the game's right-click). Its first row is Colour: Right to Blue Layer, then Enter. Down to the Input type row, Right to in beta, then Enter. The menu stays open while you change things; Escape closes it.
  • The quick way: Alt+Down walks the instruction's settings, and Alt+Right changes the one you're on. Alt+Down says Colour, red; Alt+Right makes it blue. Alt+Down again says Input type, in alpha; Alt+Right makes it in beta. The input type is this instruction's main setting, and ' changes that one directly, without walking to it: ' on the input says in beta.

Changing the colour of every single instruction gets old fast. Which colour new instructions get is the active colour, and it's red until you switch it: press L and you'll hear blue. (The game uses Tab for this, but Tab moves between stops here.) From now on, every instruction you place is blue.

Next, the waldo needs to get up to the molecule. Place an arrow up (W) at (2, 7), then an arrow right (D) at (2, 6). The waldo passes (3, 7) and gets the molecule, turns up at (2, 7), and at (2, 6) turns right, straight into the hydrogen's row.

Last of the tutorial's steps: a grab / drop (E) at (3, 6). The waldo grabs the right-hand hydrogen there, with its partner trailing behind at (2, 6).

Ctrl+B to see where that gets you:

blue waldo path

5, 7, start, heading left, 1 of 6

3, 7, in beta, 2 of 6

2, 7, arrow up, 3 of 6

2, 6, arrow right, 4 of 6

3, 6, grab drop, 5 of 6

10, 6, stops at the wall, 6 of 6

Not a loop yet: the waldo carries the hydrogen off to the right and stops at the wall.

4.4 Stepping through it

Press 0. Nothing happens in cycle 1 (the waldo just moves), so the first stop is:

Cycle 2

blue: in beta, Hydrogen, H2 at 2, 6

Press 0 again:

Cycle 3

blue: heading up

Press 0 again:

Cycle 4

blue: heading right

red: hit the wall at 1, 2

The roles are reversed from last time: now it's the red waldo, with no instructions of its own, that wanders left from (5, 2) and smacks into the wall. Notice the blue waldo passed right over the hydrogen at (2, 6) without picking it up. A waldo only grabs when it hits a grab instruction.

Press 0 again:

Cycle 5

blue: grabbed Hydrogen, H2

And that's where the tutorial (and we) let go of your hand. Off you go!
